# Replay45

NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, challenge "Field Shift: Adapting Farms with NASA Data".

Replay45 takes every crop rotation that farmers near a field, or farmers in the place that used to have its weather, could grow. It runs each one through 45 real seasons of NASA weather on that field's soil, then measures each rotation by risk as well as yield: average harvest, bad-year harvest, and which crops fail in the same years. Nothing is forecasted the farmer decides everything.

## Files

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `index.html` | The whole app in one file. Open it in any browser. |
| `fetch_data.py` | Downloads the data the app is built from. Python 3.8+, no extra packages. |

## Demo fields

- **Cozad, Nebraska**, on the 100th meridian. The main risk is drought.
- **Loudoun County, Virginia**, our home county. Rain is rarely short, so the question is how many crops a field can grow in a year.

## Data

- NASA POWER daily and monthly weather, 1981–2025
- NASA MODIS MOD13Q1 NDVI (ORNL DAAC)
- USDA NASS Cropland Data Layer, 2008–2025
- USDA NRCS SSURGO soil survey
- US Census boundaries (us-atlas)
- USDA World Agricultural Supply and Demand Estimates, 10 November 2026 (starting prices and yields for the dollars panel)

## Method in brief

A soil water balance in 10-day steps: FAO-56 Penman-Monteith evapotranspiration and crop coefficients, SCS curve-number runoff, FAO-33 yield response to water, with frost, heat and ripening checks. Each rotation is run once per starting year of its cycle and the runs are pooled.

## Limits

Results are relative crop potential from weather and soil. Dollar figures are gross revenue from US-average prices and yields, before costs, so they are not profit. There are no pests or nutrients in the model. Crop values are typical ones, not tuned to local varieties. One soil and one 250 m satellite pixel per field. The tool supports a farmer's judgment and does not replace it.
