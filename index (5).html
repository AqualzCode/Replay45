Replay45

Don't predict the next season.
Replay the last 45.

Replay45 takes every crop rotation that farmers near a field, or farmers in the place that used to have its weather, could grow. Then it replays each one through every season NASA has on record for that field and its soil. Nothing here is a forecast. Every number is something that would have happened.

The problemFarmers choose a rotation years ahead.The weather they learned on is changing, and a forecast cannot be checked.
What this doesIt tests each rotation on past weather.Every rotation a field could grow is run through 45 real seasons of NASA data.
How it is measuredBy risk as well as yield.Average harvest, bad-year harvest, and which crops fail in the same years.
Who decidesThe farmer.Every option lists its trade-offs, its gaps, and what to ask a local agronomist.

3 · Risk and return

Risk and return for each rotation

A farm that rotates crops across its fields grows a mix of crops every year, like an investor holding a mix of stocks. So the measures investors use for risk work here too: average result, how much it varies, the bad-year result, and whether the parts fail together.

In dollars

Gross revenue per acre: price × full-crop yield × the share of a full crop the replay harvested. Change the prices and yields to your own.

Starting values are US averages for 2026/27 from the USDA supply and demand report of 10 November 2026 (season-average farm price and average yield). This is revenue before seed, fertiliser, fuel, rent and insurance, so it is not profit. A fallow year earns nothing and a cover crop is not sold.

7 · Terms and data

What the terms mean and where the data comes from

What the words mean

RotationThe order of crops grown on one field, repeated every few years. Cover cropA crop such as winter rye grown to protect the soil between cash crops, not to sell. FallowA year with no crop, so the soil can store rain for the next one. Double cropTwo harvests from one field in one year, such as soybeans planted after winter wheat. Yield potentialHow much of a full crop the weather allowed. 100% means water, frost and heat held nothing back. EvapotranspirationWater leaving a field through the soil surface and through plant leaves. Hot, dry, windy days raise it. Water balanceRain coming in, against evaporation, runoff and drainage going out. What is left in the soil is what the crop can use. Frost riskThe chance of a killing frost before a crop is ripe or just after it sprouts. Water shortfallWater the crop wanted in a year but did not get from rain or soil. Bad year (1 in 10)A year that only one year in ten is worse than. Finance calls this value at risk. VolatilityHow far a typical year lands from the average. CorrelationWhether two crops have their bad years together. 1.00 means always, 0 means no link. HedgeA crop that tends to hold up when your main crop fails. Climate matchThe place whose weather a generation ago was closest to this field's weather now. NDVIGreenness measured by satellite. High means dense living plants, low means bare ground. ReplayRunning a rotation through weather that really happened. It is not a forecast.

How crops help the soil

Nitrogen fixersSoybean, field pea and alfalfa host bacteria that take nitrogen from the air. The next crop needs less bought fertiliser. Cover cropsWinter rye keeps living roots in the ground between cash crops. It holds soil against heavy rain and feeds soil life, but it also drinks water the next crop may want. Deep-rooted cropsAlfalfa, sorghum, sunflower and winter wheat reach water and nutrients below shallow roots and open channels that help rain soak in. Drought-tolerant cropsSorghum and millet lose less yield when water runs short, so they steady a rotation in dry years. Mixing crop familiesFollowing a grass with a legume or a broadleaf breaks the pest and disease cycles that build up when one crop repeats.

General agronomy, stated simply. The replay scores soil by days of living cover, heavy rain on bare ground, legumes and crop variety. It does not model organic matter, nutrients or pests.
